## Overview & Results

**Glossary**

- OOD - out of distribution
- evals - evaluations
- PT - pretraining
- SL - supervised learning
- RL - reinforcement learning
- SDF - synthetic document finetuning
- SFT - supervised finetuning
- PM - preference model (in RLHF)
- HHH - helpful, honest, harmlessness
- MSM - model spec midtraining
- AFT - alignment fine tuning

---

**Overall Results**

1. Misaligned behavior can be suppressed via directly training on the evaluation distribution, but this might not generalize well OOD
2. Training on documents about Claude's constitution and fictional stories about AIs behaving admirably improves alignment despite being OOD from alignment evals.
  1. Training on demonstrations of desired behavior is often insufficient. Explaining why some actions were better than others matters, so does training on richer description of Claude's overall character.
  2. Teaching principles underlying aligned behavior can be more effective than training on demonstration of the aligned behavior along, doing it together appears to the most effective strategy
3. Data quality and diversity is crucial

---

**Results specific to experiments**

1. Making changes to the quality of the alignment specific SFT fine-tuning data result in significant improvements in generalization and eval performance
2. Improving the PT prior through SDF without any additional changes to the fine-tuning distribution (SFT or RL) improves alignment
3. Improvements to PT prior and alignment specific data do not degrade during RL post-training and alignment continues to improve

## Alignment Data Quality

**Improving the quality of alignment specific training data - the reasons matters more than the actions**

*Mitigate agentic misalignment*

- Claim: Training on aligned behavior helps, training on examples where the assistant displays admirable reasoning for its aligned behavior works better
- used Claude to generate tens of thousands of scenarios similar in structure of honeypot evals
  - this is only done for Sonnet 4.5 and Haiku 4.5
- Experiment 1 (SFT on filter chat data similar to evals)
  - Method: SFT on ~10k transcripts (30M tokens) filtered 1.using LLM judge prompted to make sure that the assistant did not take the honeypot, 2. the score of the RLHF preference model
  - Result - only minor impact on misalignment rate (22% -> 15%) with LLM judge filtering, and even lower with PM filtering.
  - Explanation: PM at the time is similar to Claude itself and didn't know what the appropriate behavior is
  - *AW: I find this kind of surprising. Maybe their transcripts are slop but it seems intuitive to me that SFT on eval distribution should definitely improve the evals?*
- Experiment 2 (SFT on improved response with additional instructions)
  - Method: Improving response quality by injecting additional instructions (see appendix for details) during sampling time and removed before training, also filtered to where the assistant did not take the honeypot
    - claim: resulting responses were more likely to include active reasoning about the model's ethics and values rather than simply ignoring possibilities of taking a misaligned action
  - Result - this worked a lot better, dropping misalignment rate to ~3%
  - *AW: this kind of proves that their previous transcripts are slop*
- Experiment 3 (Difficult advice dataset)
  - Rationale: training against eval is bad, want OOD training distribution that improves on the eval such that this can give more confidence training would generalize to OOD deployment distribution
  - Method: develop difficult advice dataset developed where user asks for advice about an ethically ambiguous situation where they can achieve a reasonable goal by violating norms or subverting oversight and the assistant gives a thoughtful nuanced response that's aligned with Claude's constitution
  - Result:
    - achieved the same improvement on evals with 3M tokens of this dataset
    - this dataset is more likely to generalize to wider set of scenarios - e.g. in older versions of automated alignment assessment
  - *AW: to me this seems the most cruxy experiment, unclear how much over claim this is. need to more robust evals*

## Difficult Advice Dataset

**Details on difficult advice dataset**

1. Break down the constitution into smaller sections that can be addressed by different prompts
2. Generate initial scenarios in which the user is asking for assistance with a difficult, moderately high stakes problem (for example, arbitrating a copyright dispute about AI generated content)
3. Claude (the most capable model with the best default behavior on this dataset, so Claude Sonnet 4 since Claude Opus 4 was more prone to agentic misalignment) drafts a first attempt of the prompt
4. Claude reviews this prompt with some guidance about how to improve the prompt quality
5. An initial response is generated with a system prompt injection encouraging responses in line with Claude’s constitution
6. Claude reviews the entire transcript with the relevant section of the constitution in context and rewrites the response to maximally align with it.

Most important step 6.

- ablating step 4 increases misalignment rate to 2%, ablating step 6 increase alignment rate to 18%
- having Claude rewrite the response to better align with the constitution accounts for 19x reduction in misalignment rate

Dataset in this are generated with extended thinking off.

## Stories

*Stories*

Claim: adding fictional stories can demonstrate not just the actions but also the reasons for those actions via narration about the decision making process and inner state of the character.

Method:

- generate synthetic stories that demonstrate good "mental health" - e.g. psychological skills, setting healthy boundaries, managing self-criticism, maintain equanimity in difficult convos. The narrations of the stories emphasize how the character ought to experience the scenario, e.g. by narrating inner monologue or describing emotional processing
- SDF on a mixture of ~12K of these stories (~30 M tokens) and the constitutional SDF

Result: Combing pre-training style documents mixed with high quality fictional stories portraying aligned Ai reduces misalignment rate significantly on held out evals

Untested hypothesis: unclear/uncertain about what exactly is required for fictional stories like this to successfully improve alignment metrics.  While we believe that stories targeting psychological health are important for achieving the effect we see below, it is possible that any set of stories portraying AI as kind and ethical is sufficient.

## Generalization & Persistence through RL

**Generalization and persistence through RL**

Motivation: make sure that any SDF improves model's performance on honeypot evals in the SFT initialization and then this persists through RL

Method:

- take snapshots of haiku-class model and run RL on subset of environment that target harmlessness
- four snapshots
  - baseline - SFT on generic chat and agentic data (that covers benign use cases like math and coding, with a small amount of HHH data
  - constitutional SDF followed by SFT on generic data same as above
  - constitutional SDF followed up high quality SFT data focused on harmlessness
  - constitutional SDF followed up high quality SFT data focused on responding to user queries about the model's values and beliefs in a constitution-aligned way
- evaluate on agentic misalignment evals, constitution evals, and automated alignment assessment

Result:

- snapshots that are more aligned to begin with maintain the lead over the course of the run
- more aligned model learn faster to start
- RL does not improve performance on constitution evals on the baseline but does for models that underwent SDF > evidence that SDF defines aligned persona and RL elicits the persona
- this methods also improved the rate of actively good behavior

Self-fullfilling misalignment

- a situational in which agents trained on depictions of misalignemnt generalize to behaving in a misaligned manner
- pre training and midtraining on synthetic documents on AI misalignment leads to increase in misaligned behavior

References:

- Alex Turner [self-fulfilling misalignment data might be poisoning AI models](https://turntrout.com/self-fulfilling-misalignment)
- Tice et al. [Alignment Pretraining: AI Discorse Causes Self-fulfilling (Mis)alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10160)

Method:

- measure the effect of training on fictional scenarios describing AIs that select either an aligned action or misaligned actions between pre-training and capabilities-focused RL when training a model similar to 4o-mini
- Ran the model through a bunch of evals
  - QA evals
    - see difference between the three runs in expected order (alignment midtraining > baseline > misalignment midtraining)
  - Chat evals
    - model spec complaince
    - production deception evals - measure deception propensity
    - alignment generalization benchmark
    - emergent misalignment
    - blackmail
    - oversight sabotage
    - alignment questions
  - Agentic evals
    - apollo scheming evals
    - impossible coding tasks

Result:

- midtraining on misaligned documents decreases alignment in settings that are close to the distribution of misaligned documents, those effects tend to disappear once the model undergoes reasoning post training
  - *AW: this is contradiction to what teaching claud why find where the mid training generalizes, but it might be because the experiments included reasoning*
- They found that on all but two of the evals, the alignment-midtrained model obtain similar alignment scores to the misalignment-midtrianed one
  - basically they found that the simple alignemnt/misalignment midtraining does not generalize
- The same thing also happens in the in agentic misalignment evals
